Temples · Thiruppugazh · Anubhuti · Festivals

Arupadai Veedu

The six sacred abodes (ஆறுபடை வீடு) of Lord Murugan in Tamil Nadu, sung of in Sangam literature and in Arunagirinathar's Thiruppugazh.

Sacred Places

Every shrine and holy place at which Arunagirinathar composed Thiruppugazh — from Tamil Nadu across to Kasi, Sri Sailam, Kathirkamam and Jaffna.

Around the World

Major Murugan temples beyond India, where the Tamil diaspora carried his worship. Not exhaustive — these are the most widely known.

Thiruppugazh

திருப்புகழ் — "sacred glory" — the devotional verse of Arunagirinathar. All 1,335 catalogued songs, grouped by the temple where each was composed. Titles are first lines.

Kandhar Anubhuti

கந்தர் அநுபூதி — "the direct experience of Skanda" — 51 verses tracing surrender, illusion and grace.

Lives

The saints and scholars through whom Murugan's songs reached us — from the poet who was caught mid-fall to the magistrate's clerk who spent a lifetime gathering palm leaves.

Sacred Texts

The hymns and narratives devotees actually recite — armour, mantra, and the story everything else rests on.

Sangam Literature

Murugan is older than his temples. He appears in the earliest surviving Tamil poetry, two thousand years ago, as the god of the hill country.
